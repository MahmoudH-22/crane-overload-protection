The problem and the solution

Problem
Small workshop cranes and hoists often have no overload protection. Lifting too much bends the frame, breaks the rope, or drops the load. This causes real injuries and damage.
Solution
A crane that measures the load continuously and blocks lifting above a safe limit. It also stops at the top, detects a jammed motor, and lets the operator watch and control it remotely.

System architecture

Golden rule: the app only asks, the Arduino decides. Every safety rule runs on the Uno, so the crane stays safe even if Wi-Fi, the broker or the phone fails. The E-stop works even if the Uno freezes.

How it works: the process cycle

  1. Power on and self-check. The Uno starts the LCD and reads all sensors. The NodeMCU joins Wi-Fi and the broker and publishes crane/status = online.
  2. Tare. With the hook empty, the Uno saves the load cell reading as 0 kg and the ACS712 reading as 0 A.
  3. Wait for a command. The user presses Lift, Lower or Stop in the app. The command goes app → broker → NodeMCU → serial → Uno.
  4. Check before moving. Lifting is only allowed with no overload alarm, the hook not at the top, no jam fault, and a live heartbeat from the NodeMCU.
  5. Soft start. The PWM ramps the motor up over about 0.5 s, so the load is not jerked (a jerk makes the load cell read heavier for a moment).
  6. Safety loop, 20 times per second. Read weight, current and the limit switch → convert to kg, A and top/not top → apply the rules → set the motor → update the LCD and LEDs.
  7. Report. Every 0.5 s the Uno sends a data line to the NodeMCU, which publishes JSON to crane/live. The app updates.
  8. Alarm and recovery. On overload the motor stops and lifting is blocked, but lowering is allowed. The user lowers, removes weight, and presses Reset. The Uno accepts the reset only if the weight is now safe.

Motor control + ACS712 (direct analog)

Weeks 7–9M1 item 4 · Milestone 1
Goal: Lift, Lower and Stop from the app, and see the motor current in amps changing with the load. Then pass Milestone 1.

Tasks

  • Wire the L298N: ENA → D5 (PWM), IN1 → D7, IN2 → D8. Use 12 V into the driver and a common GND with the Uno.
  • Soft start: ramp the PWM from 0 to the target over about 0.5 s with millis(). Never use delay().
  • Put the ACS712-05B in series with the motor + wire, with OUT → A1. Average 10 readings, and auto-zero at startup with the motor off.
  • Wire the hardware E-stop (normally closed) in the 12 V line to the driver.
  • Build a quick test frame (a temporary wooden stand) and lift a water bottle.
  • App: Lift / Lower / Stop buttons, plus the current in A. Heartbeat fail-safe on the Uno.
  • Write the M1 progress report (outline on the Docs page).

Conversion (put this in the report)

Uno ADC: 10-bit, 5 V reference → 4.89 mV per count ACS712-05B: V = 2.5 V + 0.185 V/A × I → 0 A = 512 counts I (A) = (counts − zeroCounts) × 4.89 mV ÷ 185 mV ≈ (counts − zero) × 0.0264 A Resolution ≈ 26 mA per count (average 10 samples to smooth it)

3

Load cell + your conditioning circuit

Weeks 9–11M2 item 3 · 2 marks
Goal: a weight reading in kg through the amplifier you designed, accurate to about ±50 g, and the full overload logic running.

Tasks

  • Do the design calculations (see the Circuit page): gain, reference, filter, resolution.
  • Optionally simulate the circuit in LTspice or Falstad and check the output at 0, 2 and 5 kg.
  • Build it on the breadboard. Measure with a multimeter at every stage: bridge mV, amp output V, after the filter.
  • Mount the load cell between the rope and the hook in a Z-shape: each end screwed to a different plate, so the middle can bend.
  • Calibration: tare at 0 kg, hang a known weight (1.000 kg on a kitchen scale), and save the slope. Check with 0.5 kg and 2 kg.
  • Firmware: moving average of 5, overload with 3-sample confirmation, 90% pre-warning, latching alarm, reset only when safe.
  • Jam detection with the ACS712 (more than 1.5 A for 0.3 s while lifting).
  • 4th sensor: incremental encoder on the drum in quadrature x4 mode. It gives hook position, homing at the top switch, a soft bottom limit, a top slow zone and stall detection (step Q).

Unbox and bench-test every part

Phase 0⏱ 2 h
Goal: Find dead or wrong parts now, not the week before the exam.
You needMultimeterBreadboard + jumpers12 V adapterAll modules
  1. Load cell: measure the resistance between red (E+) and black (E−), then between green (S+) and white (S−). Both should match the datasheet (often 400–1100 Ω). An open circuit means a broken wire.

  5. Buck converter: with nothing connected to its output, feed it 12 V and turn its screw until the output is 5.00 V (test T0.3).

Stress analysis

Why: to prove the crane won't bend, break or tip over, and to calculate the real maximum safe load. The software overload limit must never be higher than what the structure can carry.

1. Load path: where the force goes

Main dimensions

2. The checks and formulas

Dynamic factor φ: a moving load pulls harder than a still one, especially when starting or stopping. Real cranes use 1.1–1.3. We use φ = 1.3, or 1.15 if your soft start works well. Design load: Pd = P × φ.

Arm (jib): bending stress

α = atan(H / L) (rope angle to drum) Fv = Pd·g·(1 + sin α) (both rope legs push down) M = Fv · L σ = 6M / (b·h²) + Pd·g·cos α / (b·h) SF = σ_ultimate / σ need ≥ 3 for wood
Treat the arm as a beam fixed at the mast, with the pulley force at the tip. Ignoring the brace is conservative, so the real arm is stronger.

Arm (jib): deflection

I = b·h³ / 12 δ = Fv·L³ / (3·E·I) need δ ≤ L / 200
Too much bending makes the hook bounce, and the load cell reads that bounce as weight changes.

Mast: bending + compression

M = Pd·g·L σ = 6M / (b·h²) + Pd·g / (b·h) SF = σ_ultimate / σ need ≥ 3
Checked at the base of the mast, where the moment is largest. Also check that the brackets there are tight.

Tipping (overturning)

Mo = Pd·g·(L − a) tipping moment Mr = g·[W·(a + x) + Wc·(a + d)] holding moment SF = Mr / Mo need ≥ 1.5
a = base length in front of the mast, W = crane self-weight at x behind the mast, Wc = counterweight at d behind the mast.

Rope

SF = breaking strength / Pd need ≥ 5
Real cranes use 5 or more for ropes. Knots weaken a line by up to 50%, so use a proper knot (Palomar) or a crimp.

Motor torque

T = Pd·g·r / η (r = drum radius, η ≈ 0.85) SF = T_motor_rated / T need ≥ 1.5
Use the motor's rated torque, not its stall torque. Running near stall overheats the motor.

Pulley axle bolt (shear)

R = Pd·g·√(2 + 2 sin α) τ = R / A_bolt (M4: A = 8.78 mm²) SF = 192 MPa / τ need ≥ 3 (grade 4.8)
Almost always passes with huge margin, but it belongs in the report.

Load cell

need Pd ≤ rated capacity (safe overload is usually 120–150%)
Never let the design load exceed the cell's rating. Use the safe overload as your margin, not as working range.

4. Choosing the final maximum load

  1. Run the calculator with your real dimensions. The rated load is the smallest "max load" in the table.
  2. Round it down. For example, if the governing value is 2.37 kg, call the crane a 2.0 kg crane.
  3. Set the software overload limit at or below the rated load. The app's max-load setting must not allow values above it.
  4. If the rated load is too low, fix the governing part first. Tipping is usually the governor, and a heavier counterweight or a longer base front fixes it.

Quadrature state table

Index = (previous AB << 2) | new AB. Valid single steps give ±1. "No change" and "both changed" give 0, and both-changed is counted as an error.

What the position adds to the crane

  • Homing: at power-on the height is unknown ("not homed"). The first time the hook reaches the top switch, the count is zeroed to TOP_HEIGHT_MM (250 mm). Every later top hit re-homes and cancels drift.
  • Soft bottom limit: lowering stops at 5 mm, a real bottom stop instead of the 15 s timeout (which stays as a backup).
  • Top slow zone: within 20 mm of the switch, PWM is capped at about 43%, so the hook reaches the switch gently.
  • Second jam detector: motor driven but less than 3 mm/s for 0.4 s → JAM. This works when lowering too, where current-based detection can't.
  • Display: "H:132mm" on the LCD and the hook height in the app.

Breadboard → PCB

Start on a breadboard to learn and debug, but plan every choice so it moves to a PCB without changes.

The path

  1. Breadboard (phases 1–3). Every circuit is tested here. Use the same pins and modules you will use on the PCB.
  2. Schematic in KiCad (from phase 3). Draw the schematic as you build, not afterwards. The breadboard and schematic must always match.
  3. Perfboard (optional, week 10). A soldered copy of the amplifier only, to check that noise is fine when it's no longer on a breadboard.
  4. PCB layout + order (week 11). Check, order, and keep the breadboard working as a backup.
  5. Assemble + test (weeks 12–13). Bare board tests first, then power without chips, then add parts step by step.

PCB design rules

Electrical

  • Motor current tracks at least 1.5 mm wide (2 A, 1 oz copper). Signal tracks 0.4 mm.
  • Keep the analog zone far from the motor zone. Route the load cell's S+ and S− as a close pair.
  • Ground pour on the bottom layer, with motor ground joining at one star point.
  • 100 nF capacitor at every IC power pin, as close as possible.
  • Fuse and reverse-polarity protection right at the input.

Practical

  • Screw terminals (5.08 mm) for power and motor; JST-XH for sensors, LCD, LEDs.
  • Label every connector on the silkscreen: "LOAD CELL E+ E− S+ S−", "MOTOR", "12V IN".
  • Test points at 5 V, REF, amp output and A0. They make debugging and the oral much easier.
  • 4 mounting holes (M3) matching the enclosure standoffs.
  • Use a socket for the INA125 (DIP-16), so a dead chip can be swapped in seconds.
  • Add a team name + version on the silkscreen, for the portfolio.
Breadboard rule that saves the PCB: on the breadboard, use exactly the modules, pins and resistor values planned for the PCB. If you change something on the breadboard, update the KiCad schematic the same day.

Before ordering: checklist

  • KiCad ERC (electrical rules check) and DRC (design rules check) pass with zero errors
  • Print the layout 1:1 on paper and place the real modules and connectors on it to check footprints
  • Check the Uno header positions against a real Uno (the D8–D7 gap is not 2.54 mm)
  • Check the board fits inside the chosen enclosure, including the mounting holes
  • A second team member reviews the schematic against the breadboard

Uno firmware

  • Non-blocking loop: a millis() scheduler runs sensors and safety every 50 ms, the LCD every 200 ms, and serial reports every 500 ms. No delay() anywhere.
  • Modules: LoadCell, CurrentSensor, LimitSwitch, Encoder (x4 ISR, homing, height, speed), Panel (PCF8574, hold-to-run, LOCAL/REMOTE), Motor (with soft start and top slow zone), Display, Link (serial protocol), Safety (state machine).
  • States: IDLE, LIFTING, LOWERING, AT_TOP, NEAR_LIMIT, OVERLOAD, JAM, LINK_LOST, E_STOP.
  • Calibration in EEPROM: tare and calibration factor survive power cycles. Add an app command to re-tare and re-calibrate.
  • Small RAM (2 KB): use the F("text") macro for strings, and avoid the String class.
